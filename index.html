The reader and the job

She has just seen one of your reels. She is on her phone, often at night, often in Spanish, and she wants two things: proof you can do this on hair like hers, and the quickest way to get in your chair. Every direction below answers those two in a different order and a different voice. All four share one set of facts, one language toggle, one open-now line and one booking block, so they differ in design and never in behaviour.

Four directions

1 · The Lookbook

Her work as a printed colour lookbook, plate by plate
Open the Lookbook

Why

The document this reader already trusts is your grid and your before-and-afters. So the page is drawn as a lookbook: a plate per technique, your photographs at their own shapes, two lines of plain copy, and a Book act on each plate.

Draws on

Vogue and Allure beauty lookbooks, Glossier's product-photo grids, Hershesons in London, Bumble and bumble's editorial pages, and Instagram's own 4:5 crop as the plate.

Does better

Proof and authority. The strongest "she can do my hair" of the four, and the one that reads most like a studio with a waiting list.

Costs

Long on a phone, slower to the Book act than the Pocket or the Stage, and it asks the most of your photography.

A calm, bound, bilingual service menu
Open La Carta

Why

The practical client, often Spanish-first, wants four answers in one glance: what, how long, how much, and when. The page is set like a bound carta with ruled rows and dot leaders, both languages on each line, and one act.

Draws on

A bound restaurant carta, Aesop's ruled product lists, the restraint of Hermès and Apple in a table, your salon's own Square list, and a hotel spa menu.

Does better

Calm, warmth and clarity. The most comfortable page for a Spanish-first reader and the best one on paper: it prints as a single clean menu for the mirror.

Costs

Proof is played down; it carries the fewest photographs and can read as plain beside the Lookbook.

3 · Before / After

One transformation at a time, on a dark stage
Open Before / After

Why

The sceptic with brassy, banded or box-dyed hair decides from one convincing result. The page gives her exactly that: a real before-and-after of one client under a draggable divider, then one transformation per screen with the technique in large type and the act one key away.

Draws on

Apple product pages, with one claim per screen and type as large as the object; Olaplex before-and-afters; a photographer's one-image-at-a-time portfolio; the way a colourist talks in a consult.

Does better

Costs

04

Recommendation

A blend is a decision when each part comes from the direction that does it best, and when we can say what the directions alone get wrong.

Build the Lookbook's frame on the desktop with the Pocket's order on the phone, La Carta's bilingual service rows and print, the Stage's real before-and-after as the first proof, and the Lookbook's warm paper drawn from your own blondes.

SlotFromWhat it decides
FrameLookbook, with Pocket on the phonePlates by technique on a wide screen; on a phone, name, open-now and the Book act first, then the plates, with a sticky Book bar.
LanguageLa CartaServices as ruled bilingual rows with duration and price or "quoted at consultation"; sentence case; state said in words.
PaperLa CartaOne printed sheet for the mirror: services, hours, address, three ways to book, a QR to the page.
HabitBefore / AfterA real before-and-after pair under a divider at the top of the colour plate, and the habit behind it: one pair from the same angle at the end of each colour appointment.
PaletteLookbookWarm paper, espresso ink, honey for the act only; a dark paper later if you want one for reels.

Why a combination

The Lookbook alone is slow to the Book act on a phone. La Carta alone plays down the proof that brings people in. The Stage alone hides how much you do and asks a lot of every photograph. The Pocket alone is small on a desktop and light on authority.

Two alternates, each one build away

FastestPocket frame, the Stage's divider as the single proof, La Carta's print. For a practice that runs on texts and referrals.
Strongest proofLookbook frame as built, the Stage's divider on the first plate, no sticky bar. For a practice growing its colour-correction work and its prices.

05

Getting clients

A page on its own books nobody. These are the moves around it, each with the number that tells you it is working.

  1. One front door. The page becomes the link in your bio and in your "Book NOW." highlight. Whether you book on Booksy, Square or by text, the client lands in the same place and you can count her. Number: taps on Book per hundred visits.
  2. Your Google Business Profile. Claimed under your name as a stylist at the salon, or the salon's profile updated with you on it: photos, hours, the booking link, a Spanish description. Searches for "balayage Richmond CA" and "corrección de color Richmond" land on a profile and a page that answer in the searcher's language. Number: calls, direction requests and site taps per month, read in the profile's own report.
  3. Consultation first for colour. The page's main act for balayage and corrective work is a consultation. You see the hair before you quote, she hears the plan before she commits, and the no-show rate on long colour appointments comes down. Number: consultations booked per week, and how many become colour appointments.
  4. The before-and-after habit. Thirty seconds at the end of each colour appointment: the same angle, the same light, before and after. The page has a slot per technique waiting for them. Number: pairs added per month.
  5. Reviews, asked for once. A text the evening after the appointment with one link. Reviews show on the page as soon as they exist; until then the space says so. Number: reviews under your name, with the first ten as the goal.
  6. A card for the mirror and the chair. La Carta prints as a menu; the Pocket prints as a card with a QR code. The QR in this proposal opens your Instagram today and will open the page at launch. Number: visits that arrive by the QR link.
  7. Rebooking. Blonde grows out in eight to ten weeks. The booking app's reminder, and the page's Text link in your signature, make the next appointment one tap. Number: share of clients who rebook within ten weeks, read in Booksy or Square.
  8. Instagram stays the feed; the page is the window. Pin the page link, keep posting, and let the page carry what a feed cannot: prices, hours, both languages, and a Book act that never moves.
